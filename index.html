Questions Reno buyers ask me

Can I buy a foreclosure in Reno with a buyer's agent?

Yes. Bank-owned (REO) homes and short sales are listed and bought through agents like any other sale. Trustee-sale auctions work differently — typically cash, as-is, with limited ability to inspect — so research on title, liens and occupancy happens before the auction, not after.

Is a foreclosure always a good deal?

No. A low price can hide deferred maintenance, liens or a repair bill that erases the discount. That's why every serious candidate gets a deal sheet — and why I'll tell you when to walk away.

What's the difference between a short sale, an REO and an auction?

A short sale is the owner selling for less than they owe, with lender approval. An REO is a property the lender already took back and is selling. An auction (trustee sale) is the foreclosure sale itself. Each has different timelines, risks and negotiating room.

How do you find properties other buyers overlook?

I watch price history, days on market, fallen escrows, poorly marketed listings and distressed channels — then compare each against real comps. The best opportunities usually look unremarkable at first glance.

Do you work with investors as well as homebuyers?

Both. Owner-occupants who want equity on day one and investors looking for rentals, flips, land or manufactured-home opportunities across Reno, Sparks and Northern Nevada.

How does buyer's agent compensation work?

We'll agree on it in writing before we tour. In many transactions part or all of it is negotiated from the seller — we'll talk through your options up front so there are no surprises.
